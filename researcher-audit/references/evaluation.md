# Capability evaluation

Evaluate the public record against the baseline the user names (for example "PhD year 1"). If the person's degree stage is unknown, state which stage you assumed.

## Dimensions

Rate each dimension **below / at / above baseline / unproven**, and cite the evidence.

1. **Output and venues.** Number of papers and journal tier, relative to the baseline.
2. **Project ownership.** Does any first-author or co-first original research paper show the person ran a project from design to validation? Middle authorship across many projects suggests they contributed one component, such as modeling, synthesis or assays. Which component is unknown without contribution statements.
3. **Field vision.** Reviews written and breadth of topics. Reviews show the ability to synthesize a field, not experimental ability.
4. **Independence**, the main indicator of the ceiling. Look for any of the following:
   - a first-author original paper;
   - corresponding authorship;
   - a paper without the likely PI.

   If there are none, independence is **unproven, not absent**. That is normal early in a PhD.
5. **Direction.** Is the specialty the user describes backed by original work, or only by a review?

## Authorship weighting

From strongest to weakest evidence of the person's own contribution:

sole-first original > co-first original > sole-first review > middle original > middle review

- Count co-first only when it is verified in the full text. `unknown` is not co-first.
- Do not infer shares of contribution from rank among the middle authors.
- A two-author review with the PI shows writing and synthesis. Do not score it as experimental output.

## Potential

State potential as a range relative to the baseline. Name concrete events that would raise it, such as a first-author original paper in the stated specialty, or continued output after leaving the lab. Name what would lower it, such as remaining a middle author only.

## Tone

- Evaluate the public record, not the person.
- Never infer misconduct from the presence or absence of an integrity signal.
- Database errors, such as a lost co-first footnote or a merged ID, are not the person's fault. Say so when they affected the picture.
